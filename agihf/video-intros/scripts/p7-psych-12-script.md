🧠 Psychology Lesson 12: Blowing an Account / Breaking Your Rules — “Okay… Now What?”
Target: 6–8 minutes
Purpose: Help traders deal with what happens after a major trading mistake without turning shame, frustration, or urgency into another cycle of bad decisions. The goal isn't to pretend blowing an account is normal or harmless—it's to figure out what behavior actually caused it and what has to change before you trade again.

1. PUT THEM IN THE MOMENT
~45 seconds

“Okay.

You blew the account.

Or maybe you didn't completely blow it, but you broke your rules badly enough that you're sitting there afterward like…

What the hell did I just do?

And the worst part?

You probably knew better.

Maybe you were green earlier.

Maybe you hit your daily loss limit and kept trading.

Maybe you increased your size.

Maybe you revenge traded.

Maybe one bad decision turned into five.

And now the session is over, the damage is done, and you're sitting there replaying everything in your head.

So… now what?”

Then:

“Because what you do AFTER you completely break your rules matters just as much as understanding how you got there.”

2. WHAT'S ACTUALLY HAPPENING?
~1–2 minutes

“Usually, an account blow isn't one decision.”

That's the first thing I'd teach.

“We like to look back and find THE trade.

‘This is the trade that blew my account.’

But most of the time, there was a chain of decisions that happened before that.”

Maybe:

You took a loss.

Then immediately took another trade.

Then increased size.

Then moved your stop.

Then hit your daily limit.

Then ignored the limit.

Then took another trade because now you really needed the money back.

“So the account blowing might've been the final outcome.

But psychologically, the breakdown started way before the last trade.”

That's what you want them looking for.

“Where was the first moment you knew you were no longer trading according to your plan?”

Because THAT is usually the moment that matters most.

3. MY EXPERIENCE
~1–2 minutes

This is where your own story makes this lesson powerful.

“I'm teaching this one from very real experience because I've blown accounts.

And I've even had days where I was profitable first.”

Then:

“And that's probably one of the most frustrating versions of it.

Because you can look back and literally see the point where you could've been done.”

You made money.

You took base hits.

You had a decent session.

“And then somewhere along the way, something shifted.

Maybe I took another trade.

Then another.

Maybe my risk changed.

Maybe I started trying to recover something.

And now I've gone from having a perfectly fine trading day to blowing the account.”

Then hit them with the important part:

“It would be easy for me to just say, ‘Damn, I need more discipline.’

But that doesn't actually teach me anything.”

Because “be more disciplined” isn't a plan.

“I need to know exactly where my behavior changed.”

Was it trade three?

Was it after your first full stop?

Was it after you gave back some profit?

Was it when you increased size?

“Because if I don't identify that moment, I'll buy another account and bring the exact same trader with me.”

That's the line.

4. WHAT DO WE DO INSTEAD?
~2 minutes

“The first thing I DON'T want you doing after blowing an account is immediately trying to make yourself feel better by getting another account and trading again.”

Because now you're emotionally activated.

“You're embarrassed.

You're frustrated.

You want another chance.

You want to prove that wasn't really you.

And that's exactly when you probably don't need another Buy or Sell button in front of you.” 😂

Instead:

“Do an account autopsy.”

I actually think Account Autopsy could become a worksheet inside AGHF University.

Have them answer:

What was my P&L before things started going wrong?

What was the first rule I broke?

What happened immediately before I broke it?

What emotion was I experiencing?

Did my size change?

Did my setup quality change?

Did I violate my daily stop?

At what exact point should my session have ended?

What rule would've prevented everything that happened afterward?

Then:

“And notice I'm not asking, ‘What indicator would've saved me?’

Because if the problem was that you ignored your daily loss limit, another indicator isn't fixing that.”

😂

Then create a prevention rule.

“If I blew the account because I kept trading after my third trade, maybe my new rule is a hard three-trade maximum.

If I increased size after losing, maybe my size is locked for the entire session.

If I ignored my daily loss limit, maybe I need a platform-level lockout if that's available.”

The correction needs to match the behavior.

5. YOUR RULE
~30–45 seconds

🧠 YOUR RULE
I don't restart until I understand what broke.

Then:

“I'm not buying another account just to repeat the same cycle.

I'm not blaming my strategy if I didn't follow it.

And I'm not telling myself I'll ‘just do better next time.’

I need to identify what happened and put something measurable in place to prevent it from happening the same way again.”

And your memorable line:

“A new account does not create a new trader.”

That's the one.

Because you can buy ten more evaluations.

“If I bring the same behavior into every one of them, I'm going to keep getting some version of the same result.”

6. TRADER REFLECTION
~1 minute

✍🏽 Trader Reflection
“If you've ever blown an account—or had a day where you completely abandoned your rules—I want you to go back to that day.”

But don't start with the final trade.

“Go backwards.”

Ask:

Where did the session actually start going wrong?

“What was the first rule you broke?

Not the biggest rule.

The first one.”

Then:

“What happened immediately before that?”

A loss?

A win?

Giving back profit?

Missing an entry?

Getting close to your payout?

Trying to hit a daily goal?

Then the most important question:

“What single rule, if I had actually followed it, would've stopped the rest of that day from happening?”

That's what they write down.

Then close:

“Blowing an account doesn't need to become part of your identity as a trader.

But it does need to become information.

Don't just say, ‘I'm never doing that again.’

Figure out what that actually was.

Find the first broken rule.

Find the trigger.

Build the correction.

Because if you're going to pay tuition to the market, at least get the damn lesson. 😂

**A new account doesn't create a new trader.

New behavior does.**”

CUT. 🎬
