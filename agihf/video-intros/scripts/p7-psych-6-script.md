# 🧠 Psychology Lesson 6: Fear of Giving Back Profit — “I’m Green… I Don’t Want to Lose It”

**Target:** 5–7 minutes  
**Purpose:** Help traders distinguish between **intentional trade management** and closing or interfering with a trade simply because seeing unrealized profit fluctuate makes them uncomfortable.

---

### 1. PUT THEM IN THE MOMENT
**~30–45 seconds**

> “Okay. You're in a trade.
>
> Your entry was good. Price starts moving in your direction.
>
> You're up $100… $200… maybe $300.
>
> And now you're watching that P&L.
>
> Price pulls back a little bit.
>
> Now you're up $250.
>
> Then $220.
>
> And immediately you're thinking:
>
> *Nope. Let me take this. I'm not about to give all this money back.* 😂
>
> So you close the trade.
>
> And then… price turns around and goes directly to your original target.”

And now you're mad.

> “But here's the question:
>
> **Did something actually change in the trade—or did seeing your profit decrease make you uncomfortable?**
>
> That's what we're talking about today: the fear of giving back profit.”

---

### 2. WHAT'S ACTUALLY HAPPENING?
**~1–2 minutes**

> “Here's what makes this one tricky:
>
> Taking profit early is **not automatically bad trading.**
>
> Taking partials isn't bad.
>
> Moving to breakeven isn't bad.
>
> Taking base hits isn't bad.
>
> Those can all be completely legitimate ways to manage a trade.”

The issue is **why** you're doing it.

> “If your trading plan says, ‘When this happens, I secure profit,’ that's trade management.
>
> But if your plan says one thing and then you start changing everything because you're watching $400 turn into $350… $300… $275…
>
> now we need to look at what's actually making the decision.”

And here's the psychological part:

> “Once you're in profit, that unrealized money starts feeling like **your money.**
>
> You haven't actually realized it yet, but mentally you've already claimed it.”

So when it fluctuates:

> “It doesn't feel like the trade is pulling back.
>
> It feels like you're **losing money you already made.**”

That's why it can become so uncomfortable.

---

### 3. MY EXPERIENCE
**~1–2 minutes**

This one fits your recent trading experience perfectly.

> “I've dealt with this a lot because I'm actually somebody who likes taking base hits.
>
> There have been days where I've taken multiple trades, secured profit instead of holding every single trade to full TP, and been completely happy with that decision.”

Then make the distinction:

> “And I had to realize that there's nothing inherently wrong with that.
>
> I don't have to hold every trade to full TP just so I can say I did.
>
> **But I do need to know why I'm exiting.**”

Then:

> “Because I've also had trades where I got out, watched price go directly to my original target, and then started beating myself up like, ‘Damn, I should've held it.’
>
> But hindsight makes everything look obvious.”

That's important.

> “I don't want to judge my management decision solely based on what price did **after I exited.**
>
> I want to ask:
>
> **Did I follow my management plan?**”

Because if you intentionally took a base hit according to your rules and price later ran another 100 points:

> “That doesn't suddenly make your exit wrong.”

And vice versa:

> “If you panic-closed a trade and it happened to reverse immediately afterward, that doesn't automatically make panic-closing a good system.”

Outcome ≠ execution again.

---

### 4. WHAT DO WE DO INSTEAD?
**~1–2 minutes**

> “The biggest thing here is that you need some idea of how you're going to manage the trade **before you're staring at green money.**”

Because $0 feels different from $500 sitting on your screen. 😂

So decide beforehand:

**When do I take partials?**

**When do I move to breakeven?**

**What market condition allows me to secure a base hit?**

**What tells me the trade is actually weakening?**

**When am I willing to hold for full TP?**

Then:

> “That doesn't mean your management can never change. The market gives us new information.
>
> But there needs to be a difference between:
>
> **‘Price gave me a reason to manage this trade’**
>
> and
>
> **‘The P&L made me nervous.’**”

That's probably the core lesson.

And I'd give them a practical trick:

> “If you notice that watching your dollar amount is causing you to interfere with trades, stop staring at the P&L.
>
> Watch the chart.
>
> Your chart should be telling you whether the trade is still valid—not the fact that you were up $347 twelve seconds ago.”

---

### 5. YOUR RULE
**~30 seconds**

## 🧠 YOUR RULE

**I manage the trade based on my plan—not my P&L.**

Then:

> “If my plan tells me to take the base hit, I take it.
>
> If my plan tells me to take partials, I take them.
>
> If my rules tell me to move to breakeven, I move to breakeven.
>
> If my plan says hold, I give the trade room to work.”

And your memorable line:

> **“Profit protection should be a strategy—not a panic response.”**

---

### 6. TRADER REFLECTION
**~30–60 seconds**

## ✍🏽 Trader Reflection

> “Go back to the last trade you closed early.
>
> And don't ask whether price eventually hit your TP.
>
> I want you to ask something different:
>
> **Why did I exit when I exited?**”

Was there a structural reason?

Did something invalidate your idea?

Was securing profit part of your plan?

Or:

> “Did you see your green number getting smaller and think, *Oh hell no, give me my money.* 😂”

Then:

> “And if you keep closing trades early, ask yourself:
>
> **Do I actually have a trade-management plan—or am I making my management decisions based on how I feel while I'm in profit?**”

Close with:

> “You don't need to become somebody who holds every trade to full TP.
>
> You need to become somebody who understands **why they're exiting.**
>
> Because taking profit isn't the problem.
>
> **Breaking your plan because you're afraid of losing unrealized profit is what we need to work on.**”

**CUT. 🎬**
