🧠 Psychology Lesson 11: Your Relationship With Money — “I Need This Trade to Pay Me”
Target: 5–7 minutes
Purpose: Help traders recognize how attaching real-life financial needs, goals, or expectations to a trading session can create pressure that changes the way they execute.

1. PUT THEM IN THE MOMENT
~30–45 seconds

“Okay. Imagine you sit down to trade today and you already have a number in your head.

I need to make $500 today.

Maybe you have a bill coming up.

Maybe you need a payout.

Maybe you lost money earlier this week and you're trying to get back to a certain number.

Maybe there's something you want to buy.

Whatever it is—you've already decided what the market needs to give you today.

So now you open the chart…

and nothing is happening. 😂

No setup. Price is choppy. Nothing looks good.

But you still need that $500.

So what happens now?”

Then:

“That's what we're talking about today—your relationship with money and what happens when you start needing the market to pay you.”

2. WHAT'S ACTUALLY HAPPENING?
~1–2 minutes

“Obviously, we trade because we want to make money.

I'm not going to sit here and act like the money doesn't matter. 😂

But there's a difference between wanting trading to become profitable and sitting down believing:

‘I need the market to give me this amount today.’”

Because the moment you attach a financial obligation to today's session:

“Now you're not just looking for your setup.

You're looking for $500.”

And that's a completely different objective.

No setup appears?

“Well… maybe this one is good enough.”

You make $200?

“That's not enough. I need $300 more.”

You lose $200?

“Now I need $700.”

And suddenly:

“The market hasn't changed.

Your desperation has.”

That's where your decision-making can start shifting.

“Because the market does not know your mortgage is due.

It doesn't know you need a payout.

It doesn't know what your financial goal is.

And it definitely doesn't owe you the amount you decided you needed before you opened TradingView.”

3. MY EXPERIENCE
~1–2 minutes

This is one where I would let yourself be vulnerable without making it dramatic.

“I've definitely experienced trading differently when I felt like I needed the money.”

Then:

“There's a completely different feeling when you're trading and you're thinking:

‘Okay, if I make this much today, then I can do this.’

Or:

‘I need this account to get to this amount so I can request this payout.’

Or:

‘I'm short this amount, so if I can just make this today…’”

And the problem is:

“Now every trade feels heavier.”

A loss isn't just a loss.

“Now it feels like:

Damn, now I'm further away from what I need.”

And a winner isn't necessarily satisfying either.

“Because if I needed $1,000 and I made $400, instead of being able to evaluate that $400 objectively, my brain is saying:

Okay, I still need $600.”

That's where you can end up forcing another trade.

“And I've had to learn that I cannot make the market responsible for solving whatever financial situation I walked into the room with.”

That's the heart of this lesson.

4. WHAT DO WE DO INSTEAD?
~1–2 minutes

“So I'm not going to tell you, ‘Don't care about money.’

That's unrealistic.

What I want you to do is separate your financial goals from your execution goals.”

That's the teaching point.

Your financial goal might be:

I want to make $2,000 this month.

But your execution goal today is:

I only take my setup.

I respect my risk.

I stop at my daily limit.

I don't force a trade because I haven't made enough yet.

Then:

“One of those things is completely within your control.

The other one isn't.”

You control execution.

You don't control whether today's market gives you three A+ setups, one setup, or nothing.

“And some days, the correct amount for you to make is literally $0 because your setup never showed up.”

That's something developing traders need to hear.

“A no-trade day can still be a successful trading day.”

Because if your setup wasn't there and you didn't trade:

you followed the plan.

5. YOUR RULE
~30 seconds

🧠 YOUR RULE
I don't make the market responsible for my financial obligations.

Then:

“My bills don't determine my position size.

My income goal doesn't determine how many trades I take.

The payout I want doesn't determine whether a setup is valid.

And the amount I need doesn't change what the market is actually giving me.”

Then your memorable line:

“I trade the setup in front of me—not the dollar amount in my head.”

That's the one.

6. TRADER REFLECTION
~30–60 seconds

✍🏽 Trader Reflection
“I want you to think about how you feel when you sit down to trade.

Are you simply looking for your setup?

Or are you already calculating what you need to make?”

Then:

“Have you ever taken another trade because the first winner wasn't enough?

Increased your risk because you were trying to hit a payout?

Continued trading because you were trying to make back money you spent?

Forced a setup because you felt like you couldn't afford to have a $0 day?”

Then ask the bigger question:

“If you knew you didn't need to make any money today, would you trade differently?”

Pause there.

Because that answer can tell them a LOT.

Then close:

“Trading can absolutely be something you build income from.

But you still can't demand that today's market solve today's financial problem.

Some days you're going to make money.

Some days you're going to lose.

Some days there won't be a trade worth taking at all.

Your responsibility is not to force the market to pay you.

Your responsibility is to execute your process when your opportunity actually shows up.

Trade the setup.

Manage the risk.

Let the money be the result—not the instruction.”

CUT. 🎬
