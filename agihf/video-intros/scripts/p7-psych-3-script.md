🧠 Psychology Lesson 3: Revenge Trading — “I Need My Money Back”
Target: 5–7 minutes
Purpose: Help traders recognize when they're no longer trading the market in front of them—they're trying to undo the last trade.
1. PUT THEM IN THE MOMENT
~30–45 seconds
“Okay. You just took a loss.
And let's make it worse—you actually followed your plan.
You waited for your setup. You got your confirmation. You entered where you were supposed to enter. You respected your stop.
And it lost.
Now you're sitting there looking at that red number thinking, damn.
Then five minutes later, price starts moving again.
And now you're thinking, ‘Okay. I can make that back.’
That's where we have to be careful.
Because taking another trade after a loss isn't automatically revenge trading.
The question is: Why are you taking the next trade?”

That's the setup.
2. WHAT'S ACTUALLY HAPPENING?
~1–2 minutes
“Revenge trading isn't just taking another trade after you lose.
It's when that loss starts influencing what you do next.”

Maybe you increase your contracts.
Maybe you enter faster than you normally would.
Maybe a setup that normally wouldn't be good enough suddenly looks good enough.
Maybe you take three trades back-to-back because you're trying to get back to breakeven.
“And here's what I think is happening in a lot of those moments:
You're not trying to trade anymore. You're trying to erase the feeling of losing.”

That's the psychological piece.
“Because that red number bothers you.
If you were up $400 and lost $100, now you want your $100 back.
If you started at zero and you're down $300, now you're thinking, ‘I just need one good trade.’
And suddenly you're not evaluating the market based on what's actually happening.
You're evaluating every opportunity based on whether it can get you back to where you were.”

And that's dangerous because:
“The market doesn't know you lost the last trade.”

Your next setup doesn't become more likely to win because you need it to.
3. MY EXPERIENCE
~1–2 minutes
This is another one where your transparency will make the lesson.
“I've experienced this myself.
And one thing I've learned is that revenge trading doesn't always look dramatic.
Sometimes it's not me slamming buttons and taking ten trades in five minutes. 😂
Sometimes it's subtle.”

Then give examples:
“Maybe I would've normally waited for another confirmation, but now I'm a little quicker to enter.
Maybe I would've normally said, ‘Nah, that's not clean enough,’ but because I just lost, now I'm trying to make this setup work.
Or maybe I'm thinking about increasing my size because now I don't just want to win—I want to make back what I lost and still have the profitable day I planned on having.”

Then:
“That's why I've had to learn to pay attention not only to what trade I'm taking, but why I'm taking it.
Because I can make a chart look like a setup when I really, really want a trade.”

That's a good line to sit on for a second.
“And sometimes the best thing I can do after a loss is nothing.”

4. WHAT DO WE DO INSTEAD?
~1–2 minutes
“First, we have to normalize losing trades.
A stop loss doesn't automatically mean something went wrong.”

If the setup was valid, risk was correct, and you followed your plan:
“You didn't necessarily make a mistake.
Your trade lost.
Those are two completely different things.”

Then give them the practical reset:
“So after a full stop loss, I want you to create space between that trade and the next decision.”

Not because another valid setup can't appear quickly—but because you need to know who is making the next decision.
“Is this calm you?
Or is this ‘give me my damn money back’ you? 😂”

Then give them a checklist:
Before another trade, ask yourself:
Is this actually my setup?
Would I take this exact trade if my previous trade had won?
Am I using my normal size?
Am I accepting the risk on THIS trade—or am I thinking about recovering the last loss?
That second question is especially powerful:
“Would I take this trade if my previous trade had won?”

If suddenly the trade isn't nearly as attractive, you probably just learned something about your motivation.
5. YOUR RULE
~30 seconds
🧠 YOUR RULE
After a full stop loss, I reset before I re-enter.
Then define what reset means:
“That might mean I physically step away for ten minutes.
It might mean I get up, get some water, use the bathroom, whatever.
But I'm creating a break between the loss and my next decision.
And when I come back, the next trade has to meet the exact same criteria it would've needed to meet if I hadn't lost.”

Then hit them with:
“My next trade's job is not to pay for my last trade.”

That's your memorable line for this lesson.
6. TRADER REFLECTION
~30–60 seconds
✍🏽 Trader Reflection
“I want you to think about the last time you took a loss and continued trading.
What happened to your behavior afterward?
Did you increase your size?
Did you start entering faster?
Did your standards for a valid setup change?
Did you start thinking about how much you needed to make back?”

Then:
“And here's the biggest question:
If your first trade had been a winner instead of a loser, would you have taken the next trade exactly the same way?
If the answer is no, I want you to figure out what changed.”

Close with:
“Taking another trade after a loss isn't the problem.
Taking another trade because you lost is where we get ourselves in trouble.
Take the loss. Reset. Come back to your rules.
Because the market doesn't owe you your money back—and your next trade doesn't need to carry the responsibility of getting it back.”

CUT. 🎬
