🧠 Psychology Lesson 4: Overtrading — “Why Am I Still Trading?”
Target: 5–7 minutes
Purpose: Help traders recognize when they're continuing to trade because the market is open—not because they actually have a reason to be in it.
1. PUT THEM IN THE MOMENT
~30–45 seconds
“Okay. You've already taken a few trades today.
Maybe you're green. Maybe you're red. Maybe you're sitting right around breakeven.
But you're still at the chart.
And another little move happens, so you take another trade.
That one closes. You see something else. Take another one.
Next thing you know, you're on trade number six… seven… eight.
And at some point you have to ask yourself:
What am I still looking for?
Because there's a difference between taking multiple valid setups and trading simply because you're still sitting in front of the chart.”

Then:
“That's what we're talking about today: overtrading.”

2. WHAT'S ACTUALLY HAPPENING?
~1–2 minutes
“I think when people hear overtrading, they automatically think it means taking a certain number of trades.
Like three trades is okay, but seven trades means you're overtrading.
I don't necessarily think it's that simple.”

Because depending on someone's strategy, there could legitimately be multiple setups.
“To me, overtrading starts when the reason you're continuing to trade has less to do with your actual trading plan and more to do with what you're trying to feel or accomplish.”

Maybe you're red:
“‘I can't end the day like this. Let me take one more.’”

Maybe you're green:
“‘I'm up $400. I could probably get to $500.’”

Then you get to $500:
“‘Well… $600 would be nice.’ 😂”

Or maybe you're just bored.
“‘I'm already here. The market is moving. Let me see if I can catch something.’”

And that's where you want them to recognize the shift:
“At some point, you stopped waiting for opportunities and started looking for reasons to trade.”

That's overtrading.
3. MY EXPERIENCE
~1–2 minutes
This is probably one of the strongest lessons for you to tell from experience.
“I've literally had profitable days where I took base hits, secured profits, did perfectly fine—and kept trading.
And I've had to look back afterward and ask myself: What exactly was I trying to accomplish at that point?”

Then:
“Because that's the sneaky part about overtrading.
You don't have to be losing to overtrade.
Sometimes winning is actually what makes you want to keep going.”

You can talk about the confidence shift:
“You hit a few trades and now you're feeling good.
You're reading price well.
You're like, ‘Oh, I'm on it today.’
And that confidence can slowly turn into feeling like every setup is yours.”

Then get personal:
“I've had to learn that being green doesn't mean the market gave me permission to become careless.
And just because I made money on the first few trades doesn't mean the next trade deserves more risk or lower standards.”

And I'd say this plainly:
“If I was already profitable and I gave it all back because I couldn't stop trading, the market didn't take my green day from me.
I kept putting it back on the table.”

That's the uncomfortable part.
4. WHAT DO WE DO INSTEAD?
~1–2 minutes
This one needs a practical solution before market open.
“This is why I think you need to decide some of your stopping rules before you start trading.”

Because in the middle of a session, there will always be a reason to take “one more.”
“If your rule is just, ‘I'll stop when I feel like I'm done,’ what does done mean?
Because if you're up $300, maybe $400 sounds better.
Then $500 sounds better.
There's no natural finish line unless you create one.”

That doesn't necessarily mean everybody needs the same hard two-trade maximum.
Instead, decide:
What's my maximum number of trades?
What's my daily loss limit?
Do I have a profit-protection rule?
What tells me I'm mentally done even if another setup appears?
What behavior tells me I'm starting to force trades?
Then:
“And I also want you to start paying attention to the quality of your trades throughout the session.
Go back through your journal.
Trade one. Trade two. Trade three. Trade four. Trade five.
Does your execution get better or worse the longer you trade?”

Because their own data might tell them exactly where the problem starts.
5. YOUR RULE
~30 seconds
🧠 YOUR RULE
I decide my stopping conditions before the session starts.
Then:
“I'm not going to wait until I'm emotional, frustrated, overly confident, or chasing a dollar amount to decide when enough is enough.
I'm going to know what ends my session before I enter my first trade.”

And here's the line I'd make memorable:
“The fact that the market is still open doesn't mean I still need to be trading.”

Then:
“Sometimes discipline is literally closing TradingView.”

😂 And leave that right there.
6. TRADER REFLECTION
~30–60 seconds
✍🏽 Trader Reflection
“Go look at your last five trading sessions.
And I don't just want you to look at whether you won or lost.
Look at the order of your trades.”

Ask:
“Which trades were your cleanest?
Which trades followed your plan?
What happened as the session went on?
Did your later trades get better—or did your standards start dropping?”

Then the big question:
“On the days you kept trading, what were you actually looking for?”
“Were you waiting for your setup?
Were you trying to make back a loss?
Were you trying to turn a good day into a great day?
Or were you just having a hard time being done?”

Then close:
“Your goal isn't to squeeze every possible dollar out of every trading session.
Your job is to execute your plan while you're capable of executing it well.
And once you've reached the point where you're no longer doing that, the best trade might be closing the chart.”

CUT. 🎬
