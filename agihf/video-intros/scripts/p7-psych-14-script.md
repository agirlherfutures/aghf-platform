🧠 Psychology Lesson 14: Your Trading Routine — “What Happens Before, During & After I Trade?”
Target: 5–7 minutes
Purpose: Help traders understand that discipline doesn't start when they enter a trade. A consistent routine gives them something to fall back on before, during, and after the session so they're making fewer decisions emotionally in real time.

1. PUT THEM IN THE MOMENT
~30–45 seconds

“Okay. You wake up in the morning, open TradingView, and immediately start looking at the one-minute chart.

Price is moving.

You see something that kinda looks like your setup.

So now you're marking levels while price is moving, trying to figure out your bias, checking the higher timeframe…

and before you know it, you're in a trade. 😂

There's no real plan.

You don't know what your max loss is for the day.

You don't know what you're waiting for.

You just opened the chart and started trading.

And then when you're done?

Close TradingView. Walk away. Never look at those trades again.”

Then:

“If that's your routine, you're asking yourself to make almost every trading decision while the market is actively moving.

And that's exactly when emotions can start getting involved.”

2. WHAT'S ACTUALLY HAPPENING?
~1–2 minutes

“When people hear ‘trading routine,’ I think they imagine some super aesthetic morning routine.

Wake up at 5:00 AM. Journal. Meditate. Drink green juice. Read three chapters of a book. 😂

That's not what I'm talking about.”

Your trading routine is simply:

“What do I consistently do before I trade, while I'm trading, and after I'm done?”

Because routines reduce the number of things you have to figure out in the moment.

“If I've already determined my risk before the session, I don't have to decide how much I'm willing to lose while I'm frustrated.

If I've already marked my important levels, I'm not creating levels because I desperately want an entry.

If I already know what my setup requires, I don't have to negotiate with myself while price is running.”

That's the psychology connection.

“Your routine gives calm-you a chance to make decisions for emotional-you.”

3. MY EXPERIENCE
~1–2 minutes

“I've definitely had days where I just got on the chart and started trading.

And I've also had days where I actually did my top-down analysis, knew what I was looking for, knew where my levels were, and had an idea of what would make me interested in a trade.”

Those sessions feel different.

“And the biggest difference isn't that my analysis is guaranteed to be right.

It's that I'm not figuring everything out while I'm also trying to execute.”

Then connect it to your own process:

“For me, I want to know what's happening on my higher timeframes first.

I want to understand the room before I'm trying to take the trade.”

Then after the session:

“And I've also learned that when I'm done, I need to actually be done.

Because sitting there watching price after you've finished trading can turn into:

‘Ooh, wait… this looks good.’

And now we're back in a trade we never planned on taking.” 😂

That's why a routine needs an ending too.

4. WHAT DO WE DO INSTEAD?
~2 minutes

I'd teach them a really simple three-part routine.

BEFORE THE SESSION
“Before I take a trade, I need to know what I'm walking into.”

That can include:

What's my higher-timeframe context?

Where are my important levels?

What setup am I waiting for?

What's my risk per trade?

What's my maximum number of trades?

What's my daily stop?

And one psychology question:

“How am I showing up today?”

Am I tired?

Frustrated?

Distracted?

Desperate to make money?

Coming off a big loss?

Coming off a huge win?

“Because knowing where YOU are mentally can be just as important as knowing where price is.”

DURING THE SESSION
Keep this part simple.

“During the session, my job isn't to keep creating new plans.

My job is to execute the one I already made.”

So:

Wait for the setup.

Confirm the criteria.

Take appropriate risk.

Manage according to your rules.

And pay attention to yourself.

“If I notice myself getting frustrated, chasing, increasing size, forcing trades, or doing any of the things we've talked about throughout this section—that's information.”

You may need to step away.

AFTER THE SESSION
“And this is the part people skip.”

Don't just look at:

+$500 or -$300.

Review:

Did I follow my plan?

Which trades were valid?

Which weren't?

Did I follow my risk?

Did I notice any emotional decisions?

What's one thing I want to repeat tomorrow?

What's one thing I want to correct tomorrow?

Then:

“And once you've reviewed it?

Leave. 😂”

Close TradingView.

Trading does not need to consume the rest of your day.

5. YOUR RULE
~30 seconds

🧠 YOUR RULE
I follow a process before, during, and after every trading session.

Then:

“I don't just open the chart and see what happens.

I prepare before I trade.

I execute while I'm trading.

I review when I'm finished.

And then I walk away.”

Your memorable line:

“Discipline gets easier when you stop making every decision in the moment.”

That's the one.

6. TRADER REFLECTION
~30–60 seconds

✍🏽 Trader Reflection
“I want you to think about your current trading routine.

And if your answer is, ‘I open TradingView’… we have some work to do. 😂”

Ask yourself:

“What do I consistently do before I trade?

What rules am I supposed to follow while I'm trading?

How do I know when my session is over?

And what do I do after I'm finished?”

Then the bigger question:

“Which part of my trading day currently has the least structure?”

Maybe your analysis is great, but you don't have stopping rules.

Maybe you manage risk well, but never review.

Maybe you journal everything afterward, but enter the session with no plan.

“Whatever that weak point is, that's where I want you to start building more structure.”

Then close:

“You don't need some perfect two-hour morning routine to become a disciplined trader.

You need something simple enough that you can actually repeat it.

Prepare. Execute. Review. Walk away.

Because eventually, I don't want discipline to be something you're constantly trying to force yourself to have.

I want your routine to make disciplined behavior the easiest thing for you to do.”

CUT. 🎬
