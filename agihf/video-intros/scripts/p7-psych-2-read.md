# FOMO
*I missed the trade.*

Read these lines exactly as written. Coaching notes from the script are left out.

## 1

Okay, you've been sitting at the chart for 30, 45 minutes. Nothing is happening.

You're waiting. You're being patient. You're doing everything you're supposed to do.

## 2

Then maybe you look away. Maybe you went to the bathroom. Maybe you were doing something on your phone.

## 3

You come back to the chart and price has MOVED.

And immediately you're like… damn. I missed it.

## 4

And now you're watching that candle keep going and going and you're thinking, ‘Maybe I can still get in. Maybe it'll keep running. I don't want to miss this whole move.’

And that's where FOMO starts getting expensive.

## 5

Because the problem isn't that you missed a trade. You're going to miss trades.

The problem is what you decide to do because you missed it.

## 6

FOMO is fear of missing out. But in trading, I think it's important to understand what you're actually afraid of missing.

Sometimes it's not even the setup.

You're afraid of missing the money you think you could've made.

Now you're doing the math.

‘If I would've gotten in right there, I'd be up $300.’

‘If I had four contracts, that would've been $600.’

‘Look how far it went.’

And the longer price runs, the more money you mentally assign to a trade you never even took.

## 7

And now something changes.

Five minutes ago you were asking: ‘Is my setup here?’

Now you're asking: ‘How can I get into this move?’

Those are not the same question.

Once your goal changes from finding your setup to finding some way into the move, that's when you need to recognize: okay, I'm not necessarily trading my system anymore. I may be reacting to FOMO.

## 8

I've done this plenty of times.

I've watched price move without me and thought, okay, maybe I can still catch it.

And then I enter somewhere I would've NEVER entered if I had been sitting there watching the setup develop from the beginning.

## 9

And here's the crazy part. Sometimes you chase it and it works.

And I actually think that can make FOMO worse.

Because now your brain has evidence that chasing can pay you.

So next time you're like, ‘Well, last time I got in late and it still went.’

## 10

But one winning trade doesn't suddenly make breaking your rules a good decision.

Remember what we just talked about: outcome and execution are not the same thing.

You can make money on a bad decision.

And you can lose money on a good decision.

## 11

So the first thing I want you to do when you feel FOMO is stop trying to figure out how much money you could've made.

That money was never yours.

## 12

Come back to your system.

Is there currently a valid setup?

Not, ‘Can I somehow justify getting in?’

Not, ‘Do I think price is going to keep running?’

Not, ‘Can I catch another 30 points?’

Does my setup currently exist?

## 13

Missing one entry doesn't necessarily mean you can't trade for the rest of the day. There may be another completely valid setup later.

But that next setup needs to stand on its own merit.

You don't get to lower your standards because you're mad you missed the first one.

## 14

If you catch yourself staring at a move thinking, ‘I need to get in, I need to get in, I need to get in,’ take your hand off the mouse.

Literally.

Sit back.

And ask: What exactly am I waiting to see before I'm allowed to enter?

If you can't answer that based on your written system, you probably don't need to be clicking anything.

## 15

I don't chase the original trade.

If another valid setup develops, I can evaluate that setup separately.

But I'm not going to turn missing one opportunity into taking a bad trade.

Missing a trade costs me $0. Chasing one can actually cost me money.

## 16

Think about the last trade you chased.

And be real with yourself.

What actually made you enter?

Was your setup there?

Or were you watching price move without you and getting uncomfortable because you weren't participating?

And here's the bigger question:

If you hadn't seen the move that happened before your entry, would you still have taken that exact trade?

If the answer is no, that tells you something.

## 17

The goal isn't to catch every move.

The goal is to execute your moves.

There will always be another candle. There will always be another session. There will always be another opportunity.

You don't have to turn every move you see into a trade you take.
