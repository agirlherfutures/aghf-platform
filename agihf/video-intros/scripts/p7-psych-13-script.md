🧠 Psychology Lesson 13: Building Confidence Through Data — “Do I Actually Trust My Setup?”
Target: 5–7 minutes
Purpose: Help traders understand that confidence shouldn't come from hype, one winning streak, or convincing themselves they're a good trader. Real confidence comes from evidence: backtesting, journaling, repetition, and knowing what their setup actually does over time.

1. PUT THEM IN THE MOMENT
~30–45 seconds

“Okay. Your setup appears.

And technically… you know what you're supposed to do.

You've got your confirmation. Your entry is there. Your risk makes sense.

But you're still sitting there staring at it like…

I don't know. 😂

What if this doesn't work?

So maybe you hesitate.

Maybe you skip it.

Maybe you start looking for another confirmation.

Maybe you go into Discord to see what somebody else thinks.

Maybe you pull up another timeframe.

And at some point, I have to ask:

Do you actually trust your setup?

And more importantly—what have you done to give yourself a reason to trust it?”

Then:

“Because confidence in trading isn't something you just talk yourself into. You build it.”

2. WHAT'S ACTUALLY HAPPENING?
~1–2 minutes

“I think a lot of traders think confidence means getting to a point where you don't feel nervous anymore.

Like one day you're going to sit down, see your setup, and just KNOW:

Oh yeah. This is going to win.”

But:

“That's not confidence.

Because you still don't know what's going to happen on the next trade.”

Real confidence sounds more like:

“I've seen this setup before.

I've tested it.

I've documented it.

I know what a valid version looks like.

I know what an invalid version looks like.

I know it loses sometimes.

And I know what happens over a larger sample when I execute it consistently.”

That's completely different.

Then:

“If you've taken your setup five times, you're probably going to be emotionally attached to every result.

Win: This strategy is amazing.

Loss: This shit doesn't work. 😂”

But once you have data:

“Now one trade doesn't have to carry so much weight.”

Because you're evaluating a sample, not one outcome.

3. MY EXPERIENCE
~1–2 minutes

“One of the things that's helped me understand my own trading better is actually going back and testing it.”

Then you can talk naturally about your own ICC testing:

“I've had periods where I've gone back through trades and realized, okay—my win rate isn't 90%.

And it doesn't need to be.”

You've seen your own numbers around the 40–45% range in testing.

“And at first somebody might hear 40-something percent and think, ‘Girl, that's terrible.’ 😂

But that's why you need to understand risk-to-reward.”

If the average win meaningfully outweighs the average loss, a strategy can have positive expectancy without winning most trades.

Then connect it to psychology:

“Knowing that changes the way I experience losses.

Because if I already know my system is going to have losing trades, why am I acting shocked every single time one happens?”

That's the key.

“The data doesn't tell me my next trade is going to win.

It tells me I don't need my next trade to win for my entire process to still make sense.”

That's a strong line.

4. WHAT DO WE DO INSTEAD?
~1–2 minutes

“So if you're constantly questioning your setup, I don't necessarily want you adding more confirmations.

I want you gathering more evidence.”

Start with backtesting.

“Go find your setup over and over again.”

Then journal your live trades.

Track things like:

Was the setup valid?

Did I follow my entry rules?

Did I follow my risk rules?

Did I win or lose?

What was my R:R?

Did I interfere with the trade?

What market conditions were present?

Then review it.

“Because eventually I want you to be able to say more than:

I feel like this works.”

You want to say:

“I've tested this.

I've tracked this.

I've seen this across enough examples that I understand how it behaves.”

And I'd add an important warning:

“But don't use backtesting to hunt for perfection either.

Because if every time you find a losing trade you add another rule to eliminate it, eventually you're going to create a strategy that looks amazing in hindsight and is impossible to execute in real time.”

😂

The goal isn't:

How do I remove every loss?

It's:

Do I understand the behavior and expectancy of what I'm trading?

5. YOUR RULE
~30 seconds

🧠 YOUR RULE
I build confidence through evidence, not emotion.

Then:

“A winning streak doesn't make my strategy valid.

A losing streak doesn't automatically make it invalid.

I'm going to test it, track it, journal it, and review it.”

And your memorable line:

“Confidence isn't believing my next trade will win. It's having enough evidence that I don't need it to.”

That's the one.

6. TRADER REFLECTION
~30–60 seconds

✍🏽 Trader Reflection
“I want you to ask yourself something honestly:

What evidence do I actually have that my setup works?”

Not:

I saw somebody else trade it.

Not:

It looked good on TikTok.

Not:

I won three trades last week.

😂

“How many examples have you actually tested?

How many of your live trades have you journaled?

Do you know your approximate win rate?

Do you know your average winner compared to your average loser?

Do you know what conditions your setup performs well in?

Do you know what your losing trades actually look like?”

Then:

“And if the answer is no, that's okay.

But maybe the problem isn't that you're an unconfident trader.

Maybe you haven't given yourself enough evidence to be confident yet.”

Close:

“Stop trying to feel your way into confidence.

Build it.

Test your setup.

Journal your trades.

Review your execution.

Collect your data.

Because eventually, I don't want you taking a trade because Dayli said it looks good.

I don't want you taking it because somebody in Discord is bullish.

I want you to know why YOU take it.

That's where independence starts.

And that's the kind of confidence we're trying to build.”

CUT. 🎬
