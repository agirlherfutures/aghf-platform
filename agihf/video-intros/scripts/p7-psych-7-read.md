# Greed
*Why wasn’t enough… enough?*

Read these lines exactly as written. Coaching notes from the script are left out.

## 1

Okay. You're having a good day.

You're up $300… $500… maybe $800.

## 2

And if somebody would've asked you before market open, ‘Would you be happy making $500 today?’

You would've said, ‘Hell yeah.’

## 3

But now you're actually up $500… and suddenly $500 doesn't feel like enough.

## 4

Now you're thinking:

I could probably get to $700.

Then you hit $700.

Well… I'm already this close to $1,000.

And before you know it, you've turned a goal into a moving target.

## 5

That's what we're talking about today: greed, and why enough can become really hard to recognize when money is moving.

## 6

When we hear the word greed, it sounds really dramatic.

Like you're some money-hungry person sitting at the computer going, ‘MORE! I NEED MORE!’

But trading greed can be way more subtle than that.

## 7

Sometimes it's:

‘I can get a little more out of this trade.’

‘One more setup.’

‘Let me increase my contracts.’

‘I'll stop when I hit $1,000.’

## 8

And the problem isn't wanting to make money.

We're trading. Of course we want to make money.

The problem is when wanting more starts changing your decision-making.

## 9

Maybe you move your TP farther after you're already in the trade.

Maybe you don't take profit where your plan told you to.

Maybe you increase size because you're having a good day.

Maybe you keep trading after you've already reached your stopping point.

## 10

That's when I want you to pay attention.

Because now your question isn't necessarily:

‘What does my trading plan say?’

It's becoming:

‘How much more can I get?’

## 11

I've experienced this.

I've had days where I was green, like legitimately having a good trading day, and continued trading.

And looking back, I've had to ask myself:

What was I trying to prove at that point?

## 12

Because I already made money.

I already had a successful session.

But somewhere along the way, what I had wasn't enough anymore.

## 13

I've literally had a profitable day, kept trading, and ended up blowing the account.

And that's a hard thing to sit with because you realize:

I had multiple opportunities to be done.

## 14

And that's why I'm learning that making money is only one skill.

Keeping it is another one.

Because what good is being able to make $500, $800, $1,000 if I constantly feel like I have to keep risking it?

## 15

The first thing is: stop relying on the feeling of satisfaction to tell you when you're done.

Because satisfaction moves.

You think $500 will feel like enough until you make $500.

Then your brain immediately starts showing you what $700 would look like.

## 16

So you need predefined rules.

This goes back to what we talked about with overtrading:

You need to know what enough looks like before money starts moving.

## 17

That could mean:

Your maximum number of trades.

Your daily profit-protection rule.

Your maximum risk after you're green.

Whether you reduce size after reaching a certain point.

What conditions end your session.

## 18

And I'm not saying you need some hard profit cap where the second you make $500 you have to shut everything down.

Your system might allow another valid setup.

But if you continue trading, that next trade should still make sense independent of the money you've already made.

## 19

If my P&L were hidden right now, would I still take this trade?

## 20

I can have a good day without turning it into my biggest day ever.

I don't need every dollar the market could potentially give me.

And I don't increase my risk just because I'm trading with what feels like ‘house money.’

Once it's realized, it's not house money. It's my money.

Making money means nothing if I haven't learned how to stop giving it back.

## 21

Think about the last time you had a profitable trading day and gave back a significant amount of it.

Find the point where you could've stopped.

What made you continue?

Was there genuinely another A+ setup?

Were you trying to hit a specific dollar amount?

Did winning make you feel more confident?

Did you increase your size?

Did $500 suddenly stop feeling like enough once you actually had it?

## 22

If you hadn't known how much money you were up, would you have taken those same trades?

## 23

There's nothing wrong with wanting to make more money.

But the market will always have more money available.

There will always be another move, another setup, another trading day.

## 24

At some point, you have to be able to say:

I did my job today. I'm done.

## 25

Because sometimes the hardest part of having a green day isn't making the money.

It's leaving with it.
