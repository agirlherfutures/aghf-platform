# Winning Can Mess With You Too
*I can’t miss right now.*

Read these lines exactly as written. Coaching notes from the script are left out.

## 1

Okay. Let's flip this around.

Because we've talked a lot about what happens psychologically when you're losing.

But what happens when you're winning?

## 2

You've had three green days in a row.

Your setups are hitting.

You're reading price beautifully.

Maybe you just passed an evaluation. Maybe you got a payout. Maybe you just had one of your best trading days.

## 3

And now you sit down the next morning feeling like…

Oh, I got this.

Which sounds great.

## 4

Until ‘I trust myself’ slowly starts turning into:

‘I can't miss right now.’

And now you're doing things you weren't doing before you started winning.

Because losing can mess with your psychology, but winning absolutely can too.

## 5

Confidence is something we WANT in trading.

I don't want you sitting at the chart scared to execute everything you see.

But there's a difference between confidence and overconfidence.

## 6

And that's where winning can get sneaky.

Because after you've been right several times, you can start feeling like you're seeing the market better than you actually are.

## 7

And because you've been winning, those decisions don't necessarily feel reckless.

They feel justified.

Your recent results start becoming evidence in your head that you're doing something special.

But the fact that your last five trades won doesn't change what your next setup requires.

## 8

I've experienced this too.

And for me, sometimes it's not even this huge conscious thought of, ‘Oh, I'm the best trader ever.’

It's way more subtle.

I'll have a really good session.

I'm reading price correctly. I'm taking base hits. Things are working.

And you can feel your confidence growing throughout the session.

## 9

But then maybe I'm a little quicker on the next entry.

Maybe I take another trade because I'm like, ‘I'm reading this really well today.’

Maybe I'm willing to put more risk on because I've already made money.

That's when I have to remind myself:

My rules didn't change because I'm having a good day.

## 10

A winning streak doesn't mean I've suddenly eliminated uncertainty from trading.

It doesn't mean my next setup can't lose.

And it definitely doesn't mean I should start doing things that weren't part of the process that got me those wins in the first place.

Sometimes we finally start seeing consistency, and then abandon the exact behavior that created it.

## 11

The first thing is: enjoy your wins.

Seriously.

We're not trying to make trading so robotic that you can't be proud of yourself.

Be happy. Celebrate your progress.

But don't let your results rewrite your rules.

## 12

After a really good trade, a really good day, or a winning streak, I want you to check your behavior just as much as you would after a loss.

If you're becoming more aggressive solely because you've been winning, that's something to pay attention to.

## 13

Don't earn the right to increase your risk because you had three good trades.

If you're going to scale, have an actual scaling plan.

We're basically letting our emotions control position sizing, we just call it confidence because we're green.

## 14

My confirmation doesn't change because I'm on a winning streak.

My setup doesn't change.

My risk doesn't randomly change.

My maximum trades don't change.

And the amount of money I've already made doesn't make the next trade safer.

Confidence should make me trust my process, not think I've outgrown it.

## 15

I want you to think about one of your best recent trading periods.

Maybe it was a great day. Maybe a great week. Maybe you had several winning trades in a row.

What happened to your behavior afterward?

## 16

And here's the biggest question:

Did your confidence in your process increase, or did your confidence in yourself start replacing your process?

## 17

And if you've ever had a really good trading period immediately followed by a really bad one, go back and look at the transition.

Don't only look at the P&L.

Look at whether your behavior changed.

## 18

We spend so much time learning how to handle losing that sometimes we forget:

You have to learn how to handle winning too.

You don't need to become scared because you're doing well.

You just need to keep doing the things that allowed you to trade well in the first place.

Celebrate the win.

Keep the confidence.

Keep the rules.
