🧠 Psychology Lesson 10: Separating Outcome From Execution — “Good Trade ≠ Winning Trade”
Target: 5–7 minutes
Purpose: Teach traders to stop judging the quality of a trade solely by whether it made or lost money. A good trade is one that followed the process. A bad trade can still make money.

1. PUT THEM IN THE MOMENT
~30–45 seconds

“Okay, let's say you take a trade you had absolutely no business taking. 😂

You didn't wait for confirmation.

You chased the entry.

Your stop loss doesn't really make sense.

If somebody asked you why you entered, you probably couldn't even explain it.

But somehow…

the trade wins.

You make $500.

So now you're sitting there feeling good like, ‘Okayyyy! I knew what I saw.’ 😂

But let me ask you something:

Was that actually a good trade?

Because one of the most important things you have to learn in trading is that a winning trade and a good trade are not necessarily the same thing.”

2. WHAT'S ACTUALLY HAPPENING?
~1–2 minutes

“We naturally judge our decisions based on what happened afterward.”

Made money?

Good trade.

Lost money?

Bad trade.

“But if you evaluate your trading like that, you're going to accidentally reward yourself for some really bad behavior.”

Let's say you normally risk $100.

You get frustrated and risk $500.

The trade wins.

“If all you look at is the P&L, your brain just learned:

See? Increasing my risk worked.”

Or you chase an entry and it wins.

“‘See? Sometimes I don't need to wait.’”

Or you move your stop farther away and price eventually comes back.

“‘See? If I would've taken the stop, I would've lost.’”

Then:

“That's where winning can actually reinforce behavior that will eventually hurt you.”

Because the market doesn't hand out grades for execution.

“The market will pay you for bad decisions sometimes.

And it'll take money from you when you make perfectly reasonable decisions sometimes.”

So you need your own definition of good execution.

3. MY EXPERIENCE
~1–2 minutes

“I've absolutely taken trades that made money that, looking back, I know I shouldn't have taken.”

And this is where you have to be careful with how you review yourself.

“Because it's really easy to forgive everything when you're green. 😂

You're not nearly as interested in reviewing the trade when it made you $600.”

But:

“I've had to start asking myself:

Would I want to repeat exactly what I just did?”

That's a powerful question.

“Because if the answer is no, then I don't care that it made money.

I don't want to build my trading around decisions I can't consistently repeat.”

Then connect it to Lesson 9:

“And the opposite is true too.

I've taken trades that lost where I can go back and say:

‘Yep. I'd take that again.’

Because my setup was there. My risk was appropriate. I followed my rules.

That was still a good trade.”

Then:

“That was a big shift for me—learning to stop letting the P&L tell me whether I traded well.”

4. WHAT DO WE DO INSTEAD?
~1–2 minutes

“This is why I want you to start reviewing your trades on two completely separate things:

Execution and outcome.”

You essentially have four types of trades:

Good execution + win.
Great. Followed the plan and made money.

Good execution + loss.
Still a good trade. Record the loss.

Bad execution + loss.
There's something behavioral to correct.

And the sneaky one:

Bad execution + win.

“THIS is the one I really want you paying attention to.”

Because it's easy to ignore.

“Don't let the money hide the mistake.”

Then give them a simple post-trade question:

“If I couldn't see the P&L, how would I grade my execution?”

Did I follow my setup?

Did I wait for confirmation?

Was my entry valid?

Did I respect my risk?

Did I follow my management rules?

“Grade THAT first.

Then look at the result.”

5. YOUR RULE
~30 seconds

🧠 YOUR RULE
I grade the process before I grade the P&L.

Then:

“A win doesn't excuse broken rules.

A loss doesn't automatically mean I traded badly.

My first question after a trade is not:

‘How much did I make?’

It's:

‘Did I execute correctly?’”

And your memorable line:

“I don't want to be rewarded for behavior I can't afford to repeat.”

That's the one.

6. TRADER REFLECTION
~30–60 seconds

✍🏽 Trader Reflection
“I want you to find two trades in your journal.

First, find a trade that lost—but you executed correctly.

Then find a trade that won—but you know you broke your rules.”

Now compare them.

“Which one was actually the better trade?”

Not financially.

Behaviorally.

Then:

“And look at that winning trade where you broke your rules.

What did that win potentially teach you to do again?”

Did it teach you to chase?

Over-risk?

Skip confirmation?

Move your stop?

Overtrade?

“Because those are the trades we tend to celebrate and never examine.”

Close with:

“Your goal isn't just to make money today.

Your goal is to develop behavior you can repeat over hundreds of trades.

So stop asking your P&L whether you traded well.

Your process already knows the answer.

Good execution can lose.

Bad execution can win.

And the trader you're trying to become needs to know the difference.”

CUT. 🎬
