# 🧠 Psychology Lesson 5: Fear of Losing — “What If This One Loses Too?”

**Target:** 5–7 minutes  
**Purpose:** Help traders understand why they hesitate, skip, reduce confidence in, or interfere with valid setups after experiencing losses—and learn to separate **the previous trade from the current opportunity.**

---

### 1. PUT THEM IN THE MOMENT
**~30–45 seconds**

> “Okay. You've been waiting for your setup.
>
> And now it's here.
>
> Everything you said you wanted to see is right in front of you.
>
> Your structure makes sense. Your confirmation is there. Your entry is there.
>
> But your hand is sitting on the mouse…and you're hesitating.
>
> Because now you're thinking:
>
> *What if this one loses too?*
>
> Maybe you just took a loss.
>
> Maybe you've taken three losses this week.
>
> Maybe you haven't been trading well lately.
>
> And now, instead of looking at the setup that's actually in front of you, you're looking at it through the lens of everything that happened **before it.**”

Then:

> “That's what we're talking about today: **the fear of losing.**”

---

### 2. WHAT'S ACTUALLY HAPPENING?
**~1–2 minutes**

> “Here's what's interesting about fear in trading:
>
> Sometimes we say we're scared of taking the trade.
>
> But we're not actually scared of clicking Buy or Sell.
>
> We're scared of what might happen **after we click it.**”

You're scared of seeing another red trade.

You're scared of losing more money.

You're scared of being wrong again.

You're scared of watching another stop get hit.

And sometimes:

> “You're scared that another loss is going to confirm whatever you've already started telling yourself.
>
> ‘Maybe I don't know what I'm doing.’
>
> ‘Maybe my strategy doesn't work.’
>
> ‘Maybe I'm not good at this.’”

And that's when one loss starts becoming bigger than one loss.

> “Now your previous trades are sitting at the chart with you.”

So when the next valid setup appears:

> “You're not just evaluating **this trade.**
>
> You're carrying the last three trades into it.”

And that's the psychological problem.

---

### 3. MY EXPERIENCE
**~1–2 minutes**

> “I've experienced this too.
>
> Especially when you've had a rough trading day—or a rough couple of days—you start questioning things you normally wouldn't question.”

Then:

> “And this is where trading can get really frustrating.
>
> Because sometimes I'll hesitate on a setup I know.
>
> I don't take it.
>
> And then what happens?
>
> It goes exactly where I thought it was going to go. 😂”

Now you're irritated.

> “And that's when it's really easy to say, ‘See! I knew I should've taken it.’
>
> But here's the thing:
>
> **The problem isn't that I knew that trade was going to win. I didn't.**
>
> The problem is that I allowed the outcome of previous trades to change how I executed a valid setup.”

That's the lesson.

And then hit the other side:

> “Because that setup could've lost too.
>
> And if it did, that wouldn't automatically mean I shouldn't have taken it.”

That's important.

> “I'm not trying to develop confidence because I know the next trade is going to win.
>
> I'm trying to develop confidence in my ability to **follow my process without knowing the outcome.**”

---

### 4. WHAT DO WE DO INSTEAD?
**~1–2 minutes**

> “So when fear shows up, I don't want you trying to convince yourself:
>
> ‘This one's definitely going to win.’
>
> Because you don't know that.”

Instead:

> “Come back to the only things you actually control.”

**Is my setup present?**

**Does this meet my written criteria?**

**Is my risk appropriate?**

**Can I accept the amount I'm risking if this trade loses?**

That last one matters.

> “Because sometimes what we're calling a psychology problem is actually a **risk problem.**”

If you're terrified of your stop getting hit because you're risking an amount you genuinely cannot emotionally handle:

> “Maybe you don't need another affirmation.
>
> **Maybe your size is too big.**”

That's a REALLY important distinction.

> “Your risk should allow you to accept the possibility of being wrong.
>
> Not enjoy losing. Nobody likes losing money. 😂
>
> But accept it.”

Then:

> “Once I've confirmed that this is my setup and my risk is appropriate, the previous trade doesn't get a vote.”

---

### 5. YOUR RULE
**~30 seconds**

## 🧠 YOUR RULE

**The previous trade doesn't get a vote.**

Then:

> “If my current setup meets my written criteria and my risk is appropriate, I execute my plan.
>
> I'm not requiring this trade to prove that I'm a good trader.
>
> I'm not requiring it to make back my last loss.
>
> And I'm not skipping it simply because the previous one didn't work.”

And here's your memorable line:

> **“Confidence isn't knowing this trade will win. Confidence is knowing what I'll do whether it wins or loses.”**

That's the one.

---

### 6. TRADER REFLECTION
**~30–60 seconds**

## ✍🏽 Trader Reflection

> “Think about the last valid setup you were scared to take—or the last setup you skipped completely.
>
> What were you actually afraid of?”

Was it losing money?

Being wrong?

Another loss after a losing streak?

Losing an evaluation or funded account?

Giving back profits?

> “And then ask yourself:
>
> **Was there actually something wrong with the setup—or was I reacting to what happened before it?**”

Then one more:

> “And if the thought of taking your normal stop loss feels unbearable, ask yourself:
>
> **Is this actually fear—or am I risking too much?**”

Close:

> “Your job isn't to eliminate the possibility of losing.
>
> You can't.
>
> Your job is to build a process where one loss is simply one outcome inside a much larger sample of trades.
>
> Evaluate the setup that's in front of you—not the one that happened before it.”

**CUT. 🎬**
