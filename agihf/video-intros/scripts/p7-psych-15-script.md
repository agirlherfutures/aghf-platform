🧠 Psychology Lesson 15: Build Your Trading Rules — “What Kind of Trader Am I Going to Be?”
Target: 6–8 minutes
Purpose: Bring the entire psychology section together. They've learned to recognize their patterns; now they need to turn that awareness into personal rules they can actually follow when emotions show up.

This shouldn't feel like one more psychology lesson. It should feel like: Okay, now what are you going to do with everything you just learned?

1. PUT THEM IN THE MOMENT
~45 seconds

“Okay. We've talked about a LOT in this section.

FOMO.

Revenge trading.

Overtrading.

Fear.

Greed.

Losing.

Winning.

Blowing accounts.

Your relationship with money.

Confidence.

Your trading routine.

And at this point, you probably recognize yourself in at least a few of these lessons. 😂

But here's the thing:

Recognizing your problem isn't enough.

You can know, ‘Yeah, I revenge trade.’

Okay.

What are you going to do the next time you take a loss and feel the urge to make your money back?

That's where your trading rules come in.”

Then:

“This final lesson is about taking everything you've learned about yourself and turning it into instructions for the trader you're trying to become.”

2. WHAT'S ACTUALLY HAPPENING?
~1–2 minutes

“Throughout this section, you've probably noticed that psychology isn't really about getting rid of emotions.”

You're still going to feel FOMO.

You're still going to get frustrated after a loss.

You're still going to get excited when you're winning.

You're still going to have days when you're scared to enter.

“The goal isn't to become somebody who feels nothing.

The goal is to know what you do when those feelings show up.”

That's the culmination of Phase 8.

“Because if my entire psychology plan is:

‘Dayli, just be disciplined today.’

What does that actually mean? 😂”

Instead:

“I need behaviors.

If this happens, I do this.”

If I take a full stop → I reset.

If I miss my entry → I don't chase.

If I reach my daily loss limit → my session is over.

If I'm green and want to keep trading → I check my stopping rules.

If I'm scared after a loss → I evaluate the current setup independently.

“That's how psychology becomes something you can actually use.”

3. MY EXPERIENCE
~1–2 minutes

“One of the biggest things I'm learning in my own trading is that knowing better doesn't automatically mean I'm going to do better.”

That's real.

“I've known I shouldn't take another trade and taken it anyway.

I've known I was overtrading.

I've known my risk was getting out of control.

I've known I should've been done.”

And:

“That's why I'm trying to move away from relying on myself to magically make the perfect decision every time.”

Instead:

“I want rules that protect me from the version of myself that shows up when I'm frustrated, excited, scared, overly confident—or just doing too damn much.” 😂

And this is a good place to tie back to Lesson 14:

“We talked about letting calm-you make decisions for emotional-you.

That's exactly what your rules are.

You're sitting here right now, outside of the trade, deciding how you're going to respond when those moments happen.”

That's powerful because the rules aren't punishment.

They're protection.

4. WHAT DO WE DO INSTEAD?
~2–3 minutes

This is where I'd actually have them pause the video and build something.

✍🏽 Build Your Psychology Rules
“Grab your journal, notes app, whatever you're using for this University.

We're going to build your rules.”

And I would organize them around the lessons they just completed.

When I experience FOMO…
“If I miss my planned entry, I do not chase the original trade. I wait for another valid setup.”

After I take a loss…
“I __________ before evaluating another trade.”

Maybe that's 10 minutes.

Maybe it's getting away from the chart.

Maybe it's reviewing whether the loss was valid.

When I'm having a winning day…
“I do not increase my size unless my predefined scaling rules tell me to.”

When I'm afraid to take a setup…
“I check my criteria and my risk instead of asking myself whether I think the trade will win.”

When I'm in profit…
“I manage according to __________.”

That's where they define their partials, breakeven rules, base-hit rules, whatever applies to their system.

My daily stopping conditions are…
Maximum trades.

Maximum loss.

Profit-protection rules.

Mental/emotional stopping conditions.

If I break a major rule…
“I __________.”

Maybe trading ends for the day.

Maybe they document what happened.

Maybe they need to review before the next session.

Then add one I think is REALLY important:

When I feel like I “need” money from the market…
“I remind myself that my financial needs do not determine whether a setup exists.”

Now they're essentially creating an emergency manual for themselves.

5. YOUR RULE
~30–45 seconds

Since this is the final lesson, I'd make this rule bigger than the others.

🧠 YOUR RULE
I don't rely on how I feel to decide how I trade. I rely on the rules I created when I was thinking clearly.

Then:

“My emotions can tell me something.

They can tell me I'm frustrated.

They can tell me I'm scared.

They can tell me I'm getting overly confident.

But they don't automatically get to make the decision.”

And bring back the very first lesson:

“Remember where we started: my emotions are information, not instructions.”

That gives the entire psychology section a full-circle moment.

6. TRADER REFLECTION
~1 minute

For the final reflection, don't give them just one question.

Make this their Psychology Check-In.

✍🏽 Final Trader Reflection
“Before you finish this section, I want you to answer five things.”

1. What emotion causes me the most problems while trading?

2. What behavior usually follows that emotion?

3. What situations trigger that behavior most often?

4. What rule will interrupt that pattern?

5. How will I know I'm improving?

That last question matters.

“Because improvement isn't necessarily:

‘I never feel FOMO again.’

Maybe improvement is:

‘I felt FOMO and didn't chase.’

That's growth.”

Or:

“I got frustrated after my stop loss, stepped away, came back, and only took the next trade because my setup was there.”

Or:

“I was up $500, felt myself wanting to overtrade, recognized it, and closed TradingView.”

Then:

“THAT is psychological development.”

7. CLOSE OUT THE ENTIRE PSYCHOLOGY SECTION
I'd give this one a proper ending because it's the last psychology lesson.

“And that's what I want you to take away from this entire section.

Becoming a better trader doesn't mean you're never going to get emotional again.

You're human. You're trading money. You're going to feel things. 😂

The difference is that eventually, you start catching yourself sooner.

You recognize:

Wait. I'm chasing.

Wait. I'm trying to make my money back.

Wait. I'm overtrading.

Wait. I'm scared because my last trade lost.

Wait. I'm getting overconfident because I've been winning.

And instead of automatically acting on it…

you have a rule.

That's the trader we're trying to build.”

Then bring the whole University home:

“Because I don't want you to leave AGHF University just knowing how to identify structure, mark liquidity, do higher-timeframe analysis, or find an ICC setup.

I want you to understand yourself as a trader.

What do you trade?

What are you waiting for?

How do you manage risk?

What happens when you lose?

What happens when you win?

What are your weaknesses?

And what rules keep you grounded when those weaknesses show up?

Because eventually, the goal is that you don't need somebody sitting beside you telling you what to do.

You have a process.

You understand it.

You've tested it.

You've built rules around it.

And now your job is to execute it.”

Then your final line:

“You don't need to become an emotionless trader. You need to become a self-aware one.”

CUT. 🎬

And that completes Phase 8: Trading Psychology beautifully, because the 15 lessons actually have an arc: you started with “Why do I keep doing things I know I shouldn't?” and ended with “Here's exactly what I'm going to do when those moments happen.”

The natural next step isn't another lecture. It's the Build Your Personal Trading Playbook assignment, where everything from the entire University—HTF, structure, liquidity, setup, entry, risk, management and psychology—finally gets turned into their written trading system.
